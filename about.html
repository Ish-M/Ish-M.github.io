About

Hi, I'm Ishaan

I'm a Computer Engineering student in Georgia Tech's John H. Martinson Honors Program, and I like to understand systems all the way down. I have spent time developing skills in high-level machine learning, low-level firmware, and even the design of physical CPU silicon itself through structured research, personal projects, and extracurricular activities.

I got my start in research at Emory's Bhasin Systems Biomedicine Lab, where I spent two years building machine learning tools for single-cell RNA data. The datasets we worked with were enormous and extremely sparse, which presented unique challenges for developing models and analysis pipelines to extract useful information. The work I did there led to a shared first-author paper in Frontiers in Aging.

Then, I moved closer to the hardware. I co-founded Medi-Loc, where I serve as lead hardware developer, designing the circuit board and writing the embedded firmware for Medi-Zero, our smart medication dispenser. I then pitched it at state and national competitions to earn a U.S. patent and several startup grants totaling over $15,000. Now, at Georgia Tech, I'm designing RISC-V processor components with Silicon Jackets and leading the electronics and high-voltage power delivery for the Plasma Jackets fusor project.

I also enjoy helping other people learn to build. As a counselor at WhizLearning Kids, I planned and taught Python and JavaScript lessons to students of all ages, which made me better at explaining complex ideas simply, a skill I now use in every pitch, paper, and design review I write.
